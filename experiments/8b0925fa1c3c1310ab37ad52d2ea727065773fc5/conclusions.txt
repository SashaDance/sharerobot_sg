Conclusion
----------
Splitting states and actions gives meaningful gains in Bridge, EDAN, and UCSD and a partial gain in PLEX. It directly fixes the previously discussed missing Bridge inside-bowl relation. It is not uniformly better: clamp state output and FMB actions regress, VIOLA remains wrong, and RoboSet demonstrates that graph quality cannot recover from an incorrect entity role.

This version is worth retaining as the current experimental branch because its state-change reasoning is better on several important cases, but it is not ready for the 100-scene run. Further improvement is no longer a small prompt tweak: it requires a more explicit temporal event representation or post-validation that checks state/action consistency, plus better target-role inference. Per the experiment rule, stop here and decide the next design before adding more complexity.
