Experiment: RobotSeg versus SAM3 text prompting for whole-robot tracking
Source commit: ac1942b553c7a676eb91fc314f572bf3118cb789
Scope: robot masks only; no Qwen entity extraction, other objects, relations, scene graphs, DA3, or trajectories.
Methods: SAM3 native video propagation from the single text prompt "robot" versus the official RobotSeg automatic video path with category "robot" and guided boundary refinement.
Review criterion: visual quality of the RobotSeg whole-robot mask over all 30 frames. These labels are qualitative because no robot-mask ground truth is available.
Outcome count: 8 fully successful, 2 partially successful, and 1 unsuccessful.

Automated validation
Both methods and the comparison renderer completed for all 11 scenes. Each method emitted 30 masks for every 30-frame input. RobotSeg emitted a non-empty mask in every frame; SAM3 emitted non-empty masks in 303/330 frames. Non-empty coverage is a diagnostic, not an accuracy score.

9 ASU TableTop / episode_103 — fully successful
Both methods track the complete visible robot throughout the clip. RobotSeg is clean and temporally stable, with only small boundary differences from SAM3; neither method has a decisive visual advantage.

11 UTokyo xArm Pick-and-Place / episode_12 — partially successful
RobotSeg follows the arm over all frames, but it repeatedly includes cables and small disconnected fragments around the base. SAM3 has related cable leakage but is generally less fragmented. Robot identity is correct, while the mask is not clean enough to call fully successful.

15 DLR SARA Grid Clamp / episode_100 — fully successful
Both methods produce stable full-arm masks across the clip. RobotSeg is slightly tighter and occasionally omits small end-effector/base details, but the visible robot remains coherent and usable.

17 DLR SARA Pour / episode_55 — fully successful
RobotSeg and SAM3 both track the articulated arm cleanly through the large pose change. Their boundaries are very similar and neither loses the robot.

18 DLR EDAN Shared Control / episode_86 — fully successful
Both methods follow the full blue-and-white arm through motion and contact. RobotSeg remains coherent and mostly separates the held red object from the robot; only small contact-boundary ambiguity remains.

19 UCSD Pick-and-Place / episode_492 — fully successful
Both methods track the visible end-effector despite the 224x224 input. RobotSeg is slightly tighter around the gripper and leaks less onto the red manipulated object, although a few finger pixels are missed late in the clip.

28 VIOLA / episode_0 — fully successful
RobotSeg tracks the small visible part of the robot whenever it enters or leaves the image and remains non-empty in all 30 frames. SAM3 misses four frames during these partial-visibility transitions. RobotSeg is clearly more robust here.

49 Bridge / episode_10144 — fully successful
RobotSeg finds the black gripper from the beginning and tracks it through all frames; SAM3 is empty for the first eight frames. Some robot/object contact boundaries are ambiguous while the potato is held, but RobotSeg gives the more complete temporal track.

57 FMB / episode_141 — unsuccessful
RobotSeg returns only tiny or unrelated fragments for most of the clip and misses the large visible robot after it enters. SAM3 is empty for most frames and later expands over the fixture/table. Neither result is usable; non-empty RobotSeg coverage is misleading in this scene.

60 PLEX RoboSuite / episode_109 — fully successful
RobotSeg tracks the visible simulated robot from the first frame through all 30 frames, while SAM3 misses the first two. Both are stable after entry, and RobotSeg is slightly cleaner around the red cube during contact.

62 RoboSet / episode_10976 — partially successful
RobotSeg keeps the correct robot identity but intermittently under-segments the visible arm and adds disconnected border fragments. SAM3 is usually more complete and cleaner in this clip, so RobotSeg is usable only with caution.

Conclusion
RobotSeg improves temporal coverage when only a small or partially cropped robot is visible, most clearly on VIOLA, Bridge, and PLEX. On large, clear arms it is mostly comparable to SAM3. It is not yet a safe universal replacement: FMB is a severe failure, and UTokyo/RoboSet show fragmentation or leakage. The next decision should be based on whether we prefer RobotSeg as a dedicated robot-mask path with explicit failure detection or retain SAM3 until a larger manually checked sample is available.
