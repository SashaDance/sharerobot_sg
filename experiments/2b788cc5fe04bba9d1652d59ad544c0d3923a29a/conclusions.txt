Pipeline commit: 2b788cc5fe04bba9d1652d59ad544c0d3923a29a
Frozen baseline commit: 48d934e85ddd8b93a570f647f7f5c103a0dbdd01

Question
--------
Can an additional Qwen pass improve the state relations and action timeline produced by
the existing verified-event pipeline? Two global variants were tested on the same fixed
10-scene cross-dataset pilot:

1. whole_video: one verifier receives the full video and rewrites all 30 graph frames.
2. per_frame: one independent verifier call rewrites each graph frame.

The task specifications, tracks, and masks were frozen and byte-identical across all
three variants. Their combined SHA-256 is
5ccb053fa35825c20c3d1105c7b878db0e024df25baab5134802d0e563f38174.
Therefore, this experiment isolates graph verification rather than tracking quality.

Automated validation
--------------------
baseline:    10 successful runs, 0 failed runs
whole_video: 10 successful runs, 0 failed runs
per_frame:   10 successful runs, 0 failed runs

Manual relation/action assessment
---------------------------------
The categories below assess graph quality visible in the 30-frame videos. Scenes with a
planning goal that visibly disagrees with the episode are reported separately and are
not counted as task-aligned successes or failures.

baseline:    4 fully successful, 3 partially successful, 1 unsuccessful, 2 excluded
whole_video: 2 fully successful, 3 partially successful, 3 unsuccessful, 2 excluded
per_frame:   0 fully successful, 3 partially successful, 5 unsuccessful, 2 excluded

These counts are a manual pilot assessment, not a statistical metric. "Partially
successful" means that the important endpoints or event family are present but at least
one material relation, action, or timing error remains.

Decision
--------
Do not adopt either verifier. Keep the current verified_event_timeline baseline as the
best graph pipeline. The whole-video verifier has useful local corrections (clamp false
holding, VIOLA support relation, Bridge final touching), but its tendency to prefer empty
outputs deletes too many clearly visible actions and states. The per-frame verifier lacks
temporal evidence and is decisively worse.

No further prompt patch was run because another global rewrite prompt would likely trade
one deletion pattern for another. A future verifier should inspect individual draft
claims and accept/reject or adjust their frame ranges, rather than regenerate the full
graph. That is a separate design experiment and was not started.

Scope and runtime notes
-----------------------
- Qwen ran in Docker on vkusvill GPU 2 (whole-video) and GPU 3 (per-frame).
- End-to-end pilot time, including model startup/render/validation: 366 seconds for
  whole-video and 288 seconds for per-frame.
- Qwen containers were stopped after the runs.
- 3D distance was intentionally not implemented or used in this experiment.
- Visualizations use a 2x3 contact sheet and raw RGB with dynamically positioned role
  letters; masks are not painted over the human-review video.
